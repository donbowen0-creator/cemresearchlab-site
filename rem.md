<p><span class="tag">Working hypothesis</span></p>

REM is an exploratory companion theory that asks whether the same embeddedness logic developed for criminal offending in the [Criminogenic Embeddedness Model]({{ '/cem/' | relative_url }}) applies to substance abuse recovery and relapse.

## Where it comes from

The starting observation is clinical. In my own practice as a substance abuse counselor, I see the same asymmetric pattern CEM identifies in offending, where relapse risk accumulates unevenly and residual embeddedness in old networks and routines predicts return to use even after periods of stability.

## Current status

REM is being developed as a book, *Recovery Embeddedness and Relapse Trajectories: A Life-Course Model (REM) of Strain, Control, and Social Learning*. A proposal is under consideration at West Virginia University Press.

The first tests of REM, using Pathways to Desistance and NLSY97 data, were preregistered on the Open Science Framework in October 2026, before any of them were run. The registration becomes public at the end of 2026. The results will be reported in the book. REM remains a working model, not a finished one.
