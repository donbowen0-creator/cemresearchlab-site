I am an independent researcher working at the intersection of criminology and clinical practice. My work centers on the **Criminogenic Embeddedness Model (CEM)**, a life-course theory of offending that treats strain, social control, and social learning as interacting components of a single relational structure rather than as competing explanations. I am also developing a companion framework, the **Recovery Embeddedness Model (REM)**, that extends this logic to substance abuse recovery and relapse.

I hold a Doctor of Business Administration (DBA) in Criminal Justice and two master's degrees, including an MS in Sociology, and I am currently completing a counseling degree in Walden University's Mental Health Counseling program. I am a member of the Omega Zeta Chapter of Chi Sigma Iota, the counseling academic and professional honor society, and an Associate Member of the National Coalition of Independent Scholars (NCIS). Day to day, I work as a substance abuse and mental health counselor, which is where much of the clinical observation behind CEM and REM originates.

Before that, I spent 29 years in corrections, across both penal and community settings, including 19 years as a supervisor in a correctional facility and management of the McDowell County alternative sentencing program.

## The Center

The Center for Embeddedness and Recovery Research, known until November 2026 as CEM Research Lab, is the home of this work. It is an independent, self-funded research center in Oceana, West Virginia, and is not affiliated with a university. I founded it and serve as its director.

## Research interests

- Life-course and developmental criminology
- Integrating strain, control, social learning, and network theories
- Desistance, reentry, and recidivism
- Substance-use recovery and relapse
- Corrections and alternatives to incarceration
- Poverty, unemployment, and justice outcomes
- Police accountability
- Rural and Appalachian justice and behavioral health
