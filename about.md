I am an independent researcher working at the intersection of criminology and clinical practice. My work centers on the **Criminogenic Embeddedness Model (CEM)**, a life-course theory of offending that treats strain, social control, and social learning as interacting components of a single relational structure rather than as competing explanations. I am also developing a companion framework, the **Recovery Embeddedness Model (REM)**, that extends this logic to substance abuse recovery and relapse.

I hold a Doctor of Business Administration (DBA) in Criminal Justice and two master's degrees, including an MS in Sociology, and I am currently completing a counseling degree in Walden University's Mental Health Counseling program. Day to day, I work as a substance abuse and mental health counselor, which is where much of the clinical observation behind CEM and REM originates.

Before that, I spent 29 years in corrections, across both penal and community settings, including 19 years as a supervisor in a correctional facility and management of the McDowell County alternative sentencing program.

## Research interests

- Life-course and developmental criminology
- Integrating strain, control, social learning, and network theories
- Adult-onset offending and desistance
- Substance use relapse and recovery
